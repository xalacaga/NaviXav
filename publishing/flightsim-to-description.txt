✈️ NaviXav — IFR flight assistant for MSFS 2024

NaviXav is a local Windows application that turns your latest SimBrief OFP into a complete IFR workflow: preparation, MCDU/FMS entry, weather, live flight monitoring, ground taxi guidance, official charts — and live traffic injected into the simulator.

Everything runs on your own computer. No account, no cloud, no subscription.

⚠️ Flight simulation only. Always check the information displayed against official aeronautical publications and applicable ATC instructions.

🖥️ Local-first

    Standalone Windows application, rendered through Microsoft WebView2
    Local service bound to 127.0.0.1 only — no external browser during normal use
    Settings, navigation database, logbook and chart cache all stay local
    Resizable down to 720 x 560 px, panels and map rearrange themselves
    An external browser opens only when you ask for it (SimBrief editor, ChartFox sign-in)

📋 Flight preparation

NaviXav retrieves your latest SimBrief OFP at startup, from a Pilot ID or a username, with the full route, weights, fuel, alternate, flight time and declared aircraft equipment. The next waypoint is highlighted from the aircraft's actual position and passed points are dimmed.

It then completes the departure and the arrival: runways, SID, STAR, transitions, approach and VIA, transition altitude and level, altitude and speed constraints, ILS frequency and identifier, intercept and missed-approach altitudes. Every automatic choice comes with its rationale and a confidence level.

Every import checks that SID, route, STAR and approach actually connect on MSFS data. A missing connection asks for confirmation instead of silently inventing a transition.

🌦️ Weather

Departure, arrival and alternate METAR and TAF with graphical flight conditions, wind, visibility and ceiling, plus en-route wind and temperature taken from the OFP. Live METAR refreshes every 5 minutes from aviationweather.gov — and never changes your runway or your procedures behind your back.

📡 Flight tracking

Flight phase, GS and IAS, next waypoint and distance, lateral deviation, distance to go, next constraint and the vertical rate required to meet it, Top of Descent and deviation from the planned vertical profile.

The TOD comes from the SimBrief performance profile once validated against the active route, otherwise from a 3° estimate, always respecting published STAR and approach ceilings. It is an estimate and not a reading of your MCDU: the FMS may place it elsewhere. From 50 NM, NaviXav asks you to prepare the descent; at 10 NM a separate imminent-TOD caution stays up until the descent is engaged.

🎛️ Aircraft configuration and alerts

Gear, flaps, speedbrakes, parking brake, altimeter setting, autopilot modes, selected altitude, fuel, wind and the seven exterior lights, read live from MSFS in a cockpit-style synoptic. The classic compact layout remains available.

NaviXav raises MASTER CAUTION and MASTER WARNING for gear not down on approach, flaps or speedbrakes misconfigured, strobes or landing lights off, parking brake still set, wrong QNH or STD across the transition altitude, selected altitude above the next constraint, ILS frequency differing from the plan, anti-ice off in icing conditions and fuel below final reserve.

Three safeguards keep it quiet: a rule applies only to a system the aircraft actually has, a condition must persist several seconds before it raises anything, and alerts are suspended at accelerated simulation rate. Alerts clear and re-arm on their own, can be acknowledged, and the whole system can be switched off from Flight tracking. Blinking is reserved for critical alerts and is disabled when reduced motion is requested.

Third-party aircraft: flaps, spoilers and parking brake are cross-checked across the handle, effective-position, surface-position and cockpit-indicator SimVars. A dedicated adapter reads the Fenix A319, A320 and A321 cockpit controls directly — levers, engine anti-ice, captain barometer — including with engines and hydraulics off.

🛩️ Live traffic in MSFS

NaviXav injects the aircraft around you into the simulator, from four sources:

    VATSIM — live online network traffic
    IVAO — live online network traffic
    OpenSky — real-world ADS-B traffic
    Static traffic — aircraft parked on the stands MSFS itself reports, no network needed

Turning the traffic layer on is all it takes: there is no separate injection checkbox. It is off by default, and no network call is made while it is off. Radius and aircraft count are yours to set, 40 NM and 10 aircraft to start with, nearest aircraft kept first. The source can be switched instantly from the Map or the Taxi toolbar and is saved locally.

Models come from FSLTL Base Models or AIG AI Traffic — one set or the other, never both. NaviXav only reads their aircraft.cfg and model-matching files: it never installs, updates or modifies them, and the FSLTL Injector is not required. Detection is automatic, with a manual path as a fallback, and the official FlyByWire Installer page is one click away when models are missing.

NaviXav is the injection engine and removes only the SimConnect objects it created. A dedicated SimConnect connection animates existing aircraft while others are being created or removed, so movement stays smooth; the actual result still depends on your machine, your scenery and your models. Real-world traffic needs no ATC network at all: an OpenSky aircraft without a published type gets a safe generic model immediately, then adopts its exact type as soon as the registry answers.

🧰 MSFS toolbar panel (optional)

An optional panel can be added to the MSFS toolbar from Settings: traffic source and on/off switch, model set and service status, next waypoint, time remaining, next constraint and TOD, and a button that brings the NaviXav window back — without leaving the simulator. It is the only part of NaviXav that goes into Community, it is installed, updated and removed explicitly, and it follows the interface language.

🗺️ Map

OpenStreetMap or OpenTopoMap background, complete SimBrief route with SID, en-route, STAR and approach in distinct colours, waypoints, runways, live aircraft position and heading, trail and the complete track actually flown, follow mode, fit-to-route and fit-to-airport. Surrounding traffic appears as silhouettes turned to their heading with their callsign, and a click opens type, pilot, departure and arrival airports, ground speed and altitude.

🚕 Ground taxiing

A dedicated airport diagram built only from native MSFS facilities: dark aviation background, metric grid, north arrow, runways, taxiways and stands. Named taxiways stay visible even when MSFS files them as generic path segments; unnamed links and stand-access paths are hidden until you press Secondary.

On the ground near a stand, NaviXav proposes a route to the selected runway, starting with a pushback when the stand requires one. On arrival, the destination stand remains your choice. The route separates what is behind from what is ahead, names the taxiways, announces the next manoeuvre and the remaining distance, and makes every runway crossing an explicit hold-short point. A crossing that cannot be represented safely means the route is rejected rather than guessed. Deviate, and it is recalculated from where you actually are.

Ground speed is shown on the diagram, with a warning near the taxi limit and a flashing alarm beyond it, reduced automatically before turns, hold bars and the arrival stand, and never applied while you are on a runway.

📑 Official national charts

NaviXav goes straight to the national authorities and never through an aggregator:

    🇫🇷 France · 🇪🇸 Spain and Canary Islands · 🇳🇱 Netherlands · 🇸🇪 Sweden
    🇧🇪 Belgium · 🇱🇺 Luxembourg · 🇦🇹 Austria · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom · 🇺🇸 United States

Departure and arrival charts are sorted by type, the SID, STAR and approach matching your flight are pre-selected, documents open inside NaviXav or in a separate window, and only the PDFs you actually consult are downloaded and kept in the local AIRAC cache. French SIA ILS CAT I minima can be extracted: DA, DH and RVR are proposed, never silently applied — you validate them. The georeferenced overlay is offered only where the georeferencing has been validated, and a departure chart can never be attached to the arrival airport.

ChartFox is available as an optional extra source. Sign-in uses the system browser with OAuth 2.0 and PKCE — NaviXav never asks for your VATSIM password — and the resulting token is protected by Windows DPAPI. ChartFox documents are fetched on demand, are not stored on disk and keep their "Chart data powered by ChartFox" attribution.

⌨️ MCDU / CDU card

The card adapts to the aircraft family: MCDU for Airbus, CDU for Boeing, generic FMS for the rest. It covers flight initialization, weights and fuel including ZFWCG, departure, route, arrival and approach data down to minima and RVR, with the official chart alongside. NaviXav deliberately leaves out take-off performance values that cannot be automated reliably.

🔌 Connection to MSFS 2024

NaviXav talks to the simulator through SimConnect, using the native MSFS 2024 AI traffic API: connection state, live position, altitude and height above ground, heading, ground speed and vertical speed, STD versus QNH, open doors, and the airports, runways and their lighting, procedures, waypoints and navaids. Retrieved navigation data progressively builds a local database that remains available offline; the simulator must be running with a flight loaded to fetch anything new.

When the detailed SimBrief navlog already carries validated coordinates, NaviXav plots the en-route section immediately and queries MSFS Facilities only for what is missing — a much shorter first load, with route-corridor validation and the local MSFS cache still in place as safeguards.

📓 Local logbook

After landing, a concise summary is stored locally with a bounded event timeline: flight phases, take-off and landing runway with the observed wind, gear, flaps, spoilers, parking brake, exterior lights and autopilot mode changes. Events are stored as structured data and replayed in whichever interface language you select later. Everything can be deleted from the interface, and no flight data is ever sent anywhere.

🌍 Languages

🇬🇧 English · 🇫🇷 French · 🇩🇪 German · 🇪🇸 Spanish · 🇮🇹 Italian · 🇵🇹 Portuguese · 🇳🇱 Dutch · 🇵🇱 Polish

Changing the language is immediate. Aeronautical abbreviations, procedure identifiers, METAR data and MCDU values deliberately stay in their standard international notation.

🔐 Privacy

Settings, navigation database, caches, logbook and rotating diagnostic logs all stay on your computer. Logs never contain your SimBrief Pilot ID, your username or your complete route. The ChartFox token is protected by Windows DPAPI. Outbound requests are limited to what you asked for: SimBrief, map tiles, weather, traffic sources and aeronautical publications.

Please do not publish your SimBrief Pilot ID or username in public comments.

💻 Requirements

    Windows 10 or Windows 11, 64-bit
    Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 — MSFS 2020 is not supported
    A SimBrief account and a generated OFP
    Internet access for SimBrief, map tiles, weather, traffic and charts
    Microsoft Edge WebView2, installed by the setup program when required

MSFS is not required to consult information already cached locally.

📦 Installation

The installer carries everything: the Python runtime and libraries, pywebview, NaviXav's own SimConnect connector and the signed Microsoft WebView2 bootstrapper. Nothing has to be installed by hand.

NaviXav never installs or replaces SimConnect system-wide. It ships a private copy of the modern DLL, which talks to the SimConnect service provided by MSFS. An existing SimConnect installation is left untouched.

Do not install NaviXav in the Community folder. Only the optional MSFS toolbar panel goes there, and only when you press Install in Settings.
